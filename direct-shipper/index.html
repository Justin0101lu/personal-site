Why receivers are the cleanest place to start

A broker’s non-solicit protects the broker’s customer — the shipper who tendered the load. The consignee on the delivery end usually is not that customer, and their outbound freight is theirs to award. You are also the only carrier in the conversation who can say “I am at your dock Thursday anyway.” A small number of agreements are written broadly enough to reach consignees as well; upload yours and Direct Shipper flags those.

On dormant brokers, read this part

Direct Shipper shows you dates and documents. It does not tell you that you are legally clear to call someone, because that is not ours to say. Terms run anywhere from six months to indefinitely, plenty survive termination, and a broker shutting down does not reliably end the obligation — it can pass to whoever buys the book. We surface the clause you actually signed and the date of your last load. Read it, then ask your attorney.

Common questions

Do I have to switch my TMS?

No. Direct Shipper is not a TMS and does not want to be. It reads your rate cons and hands the loads back to whatever you already run, including a spreadsheet. If you would rather not connect email, most systems can email you a scheduled load report — point that at your Direct Shipper address, or just upload a load history export.

Am I going to get in trouble with my brokers for uploading rate cons?

Direct Shipper is not built to route around them. Your uploads are used to work out what you haul and to feed facility-level figures that cannot be traced back to you. Shippers you currently reach through a broker are filtered out of your own results rather than served up as targets, and no broker ever sees that you are a customer. If you would rather not contribute at all, delete your data and the free profile still works on what is already in your account.

What is a lookalike, exactly?

A facility shipping freight that resembles what your own history shows you haul — same commodity family, comparable length of haul, equipment you actually run — where you have no existing relationship. The match comes from your loads, not from a list of companies that happen to share an industry code.

Why start with receivers instead of shippers?

Because you already have the relationship and nobody brokered it. You back into their dock weekly, the receiving manager knows your trucks, and their outbound freight has nothing to do with whoever tendered your inbound load. Direct Shipper shows which of your receivers actually ship outbound, what they ship, and whether it fits your trailers — and which ones are inbound-only, so you do not waste a call.

Why tokens instead of just charging me dollars?

Because one token means one thing found, which is easier to hold in your head than five different prices. A shipper is a token. A name is a token. An email is a token. A miss is nothing. Every plan shows what a token costs you in cents, so you can always do the dollar math if you want to — but you should not have to.

Is the free plan actually free, or is it a trial?

Free, with no clock on it. Connecting your inbox and scanning every rate con in it, however far back it goes, costs nothing on any plan — that is your own freight and you should not have to pay to see it. You also get 20 tokens when you start and 10 more every month you keep the inbox connected. Parsing, your freight profile, your receivers and your dormant broker list stay free and unlimited no matter what. Plenty of carriers will never need a paid plan, and that is fine — the loads you forward make the network better for everyone.

What happens when I run out of tokens?

Nothing breaks. Everything free stays free — your freight profile, your receivers, your dormant brokers, your outreach. You just cannot pull new shippers or contacts until you buy a pack or your month resets. There is no overage bill and no surprise charge, ever.

Do you sell my data to brokers?

No. We do not sell your data to brokerages, factors, load boards, or anyone else, and there is no broker-facing product. Facility-level patterns are only ever published once enough unrelated carriers have moved freight through a dock that no single load can be traced back — and below that threshold you see nothing rather than a number. Your rates, your broker names and your customers never leave your account.

What if a figure turns out to be wrong?

Tell us and the charge comes back. Every figure shows the sample size behind it so you can judge it yourself, and we would rather show nothing than guess. When a facility has not been seen by enough unrelated companies, you will see “not enough observations” instead of a number.

Can I get my data out?

Yes, any time, in full. Export everything to CSV or Google Sheets, or point a webhook at it and pull it into whatever you run. Deleting your data is a single control and it is permanent — your loads stop counting toward anything the moment you use it.
